If you already code with an agent, you've hit this:

  • It's chained to your laptop. Close the lid and the run dies; you can't kick off a refactor from your phone on the train.
  • Long jobs want a machine that's always on — a battery-and-heat tax on your laptop, a security project on a raw VPS.
  • Going remote safely is a minefield. Open SSH, expose a port — that's exactly how 42,665 agent boxes ended up publicly reachable with auth bypass.
  • Doing it right — Tailscale, tmux, the agent, auth, persistence — is the yak-shave you keep postponing.

Here's what you get out of it:

  • Your agent, wherever you are. Drive it from your phone over Tailscale — start a job on the train, check it at lunch, it's still running when you get home. Never chained to your desk.
  • Work that doesn't stop when you do. Persistent tmux sessions keep long runs alive across disconnects. Kick it off, walk away, come back to the result.
  • A dev workflow that's already good, out of the box. Not a blank agent — a practice. Curated, battle-worn skills for spec-driven development, code review, QA and UX; GitHub wired up; a project scaffold with a real pull-request flow.
  • Secure without thinking about it. sshd binds to your private Tailscale network, not the open internet — behind NAT nothing is ever exposed, and on a VPS it reaches the same tailnet-only end state without locking you out. Structurally not one of the 42,665 exposed boxes.
  • Yours, on hardware you already own. One command, ~15 minutes. No new subscription — bring your Claude sub or a Google account for Antigravity. Your box, your files, your credentials. Open source, MIT — no one can pull the rug.
